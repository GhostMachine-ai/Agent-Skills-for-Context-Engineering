### The 10 Most Likely Questions

**Q1 — "Walk me through the 89.6% gross margin. What's in COGS?"**

*"Our COGS are primarily software licensing — the monitoring platforms, threat intelligence feeds, and compliance tooling we use across clients. Because we serve a concentrated niche, we buy at volume and deploy at scale. Labor is largely in the founding team today, which is above the margin line. As we add an analyst, margin compresses slightly but stays above 80%. The model is built to stay high-margin."*

Key phrase: *"Software at volume, concentrated niche — that's the margin engine."*

---

**Q2 — "How did you calculate break-even?"**

*"Break-even is fixed cost divided by gross margin percentage. Our fixed costs are [INSERT FIXED COSTS] — primarily the founding team and essential tooling. At 89.6% gross margin, we reach break-even at [INSERT BREAK-EVEN CLIENT COUNT] clients. We are currently at [INSERT CURRENT COUNT], which means [INSERT STATUS: above/approaching] break-even."*

Key phrase: *"Fixed costs are low, margin is high — break-even is achievable early."*

---

**Q3 — "What's your CAC? How does it compare to LTV?"**

*"CAC is [INSERT CAC] — primarily founder time and referral-based outreach. LTV is [INSERT LTV] based on [INSERT AVERAGE CONTRACT TERM] average client tenure and [INSERT MONTHLY RATE] monthly rate. LTV:CAC is [INSERT RATIO] — which is strong for a B2B services model. And because we go deep in a niche, churn is low: clients who see value in industry-specific service don't leave for a generic MSP."*

Key phrase: *"Low CAC, high retention, niche stickiness — that's the LTV story."*

*"Why me: I have the technical background and the industry relationships. I've been inside these firms. I know what they fear and what they ignore. Why Houston: 14,000+ professional service SMBs, a growing tech corridor, and underserved by niche cybersecurity. ### 5 Wildcard Questions

**WC1 — "What's your pricing model in detail?"**
Flat monthly rate, tiered by firm size and compliance complexity. Basic tier (monitoring + alerting), Standard tier (+ compliance reporting), Premium tier (+ incident response SLA). Firms choose their tier; we scope accordingly.

**WC2 — "How do you handle a major incident at a client?"**
We have an incident response playbook for each client, pre-negotiated. We contain, document, and report. For anything beyond our current capacity, we have a referral relationship with a third-party IR firm. Clients know this upfront.

**WC3 — "What's your biggest competitor's weakness you're exploiting?"**
National MSPs sell a standard package to everyone. Our entire business is that standard packages fail professional service firms. We exploit the gap between what they deliver and what legal/medical/financial clients actually need.

**WC4 — "Have you had any security incidents with your clients?"**
Answer honestly. If none: "Not yet — and we track this. Our proactive monitoring has flagged [X] suspicious events that we contained before escalation." If yes: describe what happened, what you did, what you changed.

**WC5 — "What does success look like in 3 years?"**
[INSERT REVENUE TARGET] in ARR, [INSERT CLIENT COUNT] clients, first city beyond Houston, one strategic partnership with a legal or healthcare trade association. Still focused, still niche — just larger.

## SECTION 4 — COMPETITIVE BRIEF

### Strategic Positioning Statement

CyberTrace is a **Focus + Differentiation** business (Porter) serving one underserved segment — small professional service firms in Houston — with a depth of cybersecurity expertise and industry-specific service that commodity and national MSPs structurally cannot replicate.

We are not trying to serve everyone. We are trying to be indispensable to a defined, reachable, growing segment.

---

### Three Competitive Advantages — Unique + Essential Framework (Boehm-Miller)

For a competitive advantage to be durable it must be both **Unique** (competitors cannot easily copy it) and **Essential** (clients cannot easily live without it). All three of our advantages meet both tests.

| Advantage | Unique Because | Essential Because | Proof |
|-----------|---------------|-------------------|-------|
| **Industry-Specific Depth** | Built over [TIME] of serving legal/medical/financial firms; playbooks are proprietary; cannot be reverse-engineered without losing scale | Professional service firms carry regulated data (HIPAA, attorney-client privilege, fiduciary records) — generic monitoring is insufficient and legally risky | Clients in these verticals report that generic MSPs "don't understand our workflows" |
| **Flat-Rate Predictability** | Most MSPs bill hourly or per-incident; our model requires margin confidence and niche volume to sustain — new entrants can't price this way without our client density | SMB cash flow is real-time; unpredictable IT bills are a blocker to engagement; flat-rate removes the hesitation to call us | The "$1K is small" objection collapses when clients see 12-month total cost vs. one incident cost |
| **Proactive Monitoring** | Requires 24/7 tooling investment + analyst attention amortized across a concentrated client base — break-fix shops can't fund this | The cost of detection after breach vs. before breach is not comparable; clients with breaches pay 10–100x the prevention cost | Our monitoring has flagged [X] contained events across [Y] months — zero client breaches to date |

### Porter's Positioning

**Strategy:** Focus + Differentiation

- **Focus:** Greater Houston professional service SMBs (law, medicine, finance) — a large, defined, reachable segment
- **Differentiation:** Industry-specific playbooks, flat-rate model, proactive posture — not available from any current competitor in this segment

We are not competing on price with commodity MSPs. We are competing on value with the *threat of a breach* — and we are cheaper than one.

### The Moat

> *"CyberTrace's moat is relationship depth + industry-specific playbooks inside a trust-based referral network that took years to enter and cannot be purchased by a competitor."*

### Sustainability Drivers

1. **Switching cost:** A client who moves to a generic MSP loses their industry-specific compliance reporting and their 24/7 monitoring continuity — both create re-onboarding friction
2. **Referral flywheel:** Professional service networks are closed and trust-gated; our presence inside the network compounds with every client
3. **Regulatory tailwind:** Data privacy regulations for legal, medical, and financial firms are increasing — our compliance services become more valuable each year, not less
4. **Threat escalation:** The cybersecurity threat environment is structurally worsening; demand for our services grows with the risk

---

### Bottom Line

CyberTrace is not a commodity IT business. It is a **niche-focused, relationship-moated, proactive security service** positioned at the intersection of rising regulatory pressure, an escalating threat environment, and a segment that has been chronically underserved by every existing alternative.

The judges' framework (Unique + Essential) is our framework. We built to it. Every advantage we claim is both — and that is why this is defensible.
